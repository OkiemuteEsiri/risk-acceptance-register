# Example Executive Risk Acceptance Review

> Synthetic portfolio example. No employer, client or production data is used.

## Executive summary

The synthetic register contains a mix of approved, pending and closed exceptions. The highest-priority governance concern is an approved critical exception that has passed its expiry date, lacks current validation evidence and retains elevated residual risk.

## Key observations

- One approved critical exception is expired and should be escalated for immediate review.
- One approved exception has residual risk above the portfolio's high-risk threshold.
- Validation evidence coverage for approved records is incomplete.
- Compensating-control coverage is stronger than evidence coverage, indicating that control claims require more consistent proof.

## Management questions

1. Which expired exceptions require executive risk-owner reapproval versus immediate remediation?
2. Are compensating controls independently validated or self-attested?
3. Have any accepted vulnerabilities entered an actively exploited or otherwise elevated threat state?
4. Are exception owners accountable for a dated remediation plan?
5. Is the register integrated with remediation, vulnerability and asset systems so stale records are detected automatically?

## Recommended actions

- Escalate expired approved exceptions to the accountable risk owner.
- Require current evidence before renewal.
- Reassess residual risk when threat or asset context changes.
- Track remediation milestones separately from the acceptance expiry date.
- Close records only after technical remediation and revalidation are complete.

## Interpretation note

Risk acceptance is a governance decision, not evidence that a vulnerability is safe. Accepted risk remains risk and should be reviewed until it is transferred, avoided, mitigated or formally reaccepted.
